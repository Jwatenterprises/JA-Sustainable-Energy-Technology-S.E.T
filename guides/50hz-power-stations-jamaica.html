Will a US power station work in Jamaica?

By the S.E.T. Jamaica team · Specs checked against manufacturer manuals and spec pages, October 2026

Short answer: only if it's rated for 50Hz. Jamaica's grid runs at 110V and 50Hz, with the same US-style plugs. The voltage and plug match US gear, but the US runs at 60Hz. A US power station is safe on JPS power only if (1) its AC charging input is rated 50/60Hz, and (2) its AC output can be set to 50Hz for motors like fridges and fans.

Why 50Hz vs 60Hz matters

Hertz (Hz) is how many times per second AC power alternates. Most of the Americas run at 60Hz; Jamaica, like the UK, runs at 50Hz. A power station deals with grid power in two places:

  • Charging from the wall (AC input). If the charger is only rated for 60Hz, plugging it into a 50Hz JPS outlet is outside its rating. It may run hot, charge slowly, throw errors or fail, and the manufacturer can refuse a warranty claim.
  • Powering your appliances (AC output). Phones, laptops, LED lights and TVs generally don't care. But motors and compressors, like a fridge, freezer, standing fan or pump, are designed around the frequency they were built for. A unit stuck on 60Hz output runs Jamaican 50Hz appliances off-spec.
  • Pass-through / UPS mode. When the station is plugged in and powering things at the same time, grid power passes straight through, so the input must accept 50Hz.

3 checks before you buy

  1. Read the AC input rating on the unit's label or spec sheet. You want to see 50/60Hz (or 50Hz). "60Hz" alone is a red flag for Jamaica.
  2. Check the AC output. Look for 50Hz/60Hz switchable and pure sine wave. Ask the seller to show you how to set it to 50Hz.
  3. Ask where the warranty is honoured. If it's only valid in the US, a fault means shipping a heavy lithium battery back yourself.

The warranty problem with US-bought units

Even a 50Hz-rated US unit has a catch: many brands only honour the warranty in the country of purchase. EcoFlow's US warranty, for example, says it is valid only on products purchased and used in the United States; repairs from other regions are a paid service, with duties, taxes and shipping on the customer. If you buy a big brand, buy it from an authorised seller in Jamaica.

Shipping a power station yourself? Lithium batteries are classed as dangerous goods. Many couriers and barrel shippers won't carry them, and air rules limit how they ship. That's a big part of why buying locally stocked is easier.
