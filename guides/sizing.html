Sizing guide

What size power station or battery do I need?

By the S.E.T. Jamaica team · Typical appliance figures; check the label on your own appliances for exact numbers

Short answer: to keep a fridge, a fan, a few lights, the TV, router and phones going through an evening outage (about 8 hours), plan on roughly 2–2.5 kWh of battery and at least 1,500 W of output (a fridge needs a start-up surge). Our ~2 kWh / 2,400 W power station covers most evening outages; for longer ones, add a solar panel to recharge in the day. The calculator below starts with that list, so adjust it for your home.

Two numbers that matter

  • Watt-hours (Wh) = how long it lasts. A 2,000 Wh battery can run a 100 W load for roughly 17 hours after conversion losses.
  • Watts (W) = how much it can run at once. Add up everything running together. Fridges and pumps also need a short start-up surge of 2–3× their running watts.

Questions

What size power station do I need for a fridge and fans in an outage?

For a fridge, a fan, a few lights, the TV, router and phones through an evening outage of about 8 hours, plan on roughly 2 to 2.5 kWh of battery and at least 1,500 W of output, because a fridge needs a start-up surge.

What is the difference between watt-hours and watts?

Watt-hours (Wh) tell you how long a battery lasts: a 2,000 Wh battery runs a 100 W load for roughly 17 hours after conversion losses. Watts (W) tell you how much it can run at once, so add up everything running together.

Can a power station run an air conditioner?

We don't recommend it. AC units, electric water heaters and stoves draw too much power for too long. For whole-home backup you need a home battery with a solar inverter.
