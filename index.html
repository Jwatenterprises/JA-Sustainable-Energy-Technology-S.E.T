Why "50Hz-verified" matters

Solar in Jamaica: quick answers

Questions Jamaicans ask us most

How many peak sun hours does Jamaica get?

About 5.2 to 5.7 peak sun hours a day on average, depending on the parish (NASA POWER satellite data). Clarendon, Kingston, St Catherine, St Thomas, Westmoreland and Hanover sit at the high end; the cloudier north-east and the hills average closer to 5.2. Even the dullest month stays around 4 peak sun hours.

Will a US solar generator work in Jamaica?

Only if it is rated for 50Hz. Jamaica uses US-style plugs at 110V, but JPS power runs at 50Hz, and many US models output 60Hz or won't charge properly. Every model we list is rated by its manufacturer to charge on 50Hz and output 110–120V at 50Hz.

How much solar do I need to run a fridge in Jamaica?

A typical fridge uses about 2.6 kWh a day. With around 5.5 peak sun hours, that takes roughly 650 W of solar panels plus a battery or power station of at least 2 kWh to keep it cold overnight. Use the calculator above for your exact appliances and parish.

How do I get a solar generator delivered to Jamaica?

Buy it at the US store price through our link, have it delivered to a Florida address, and drop it at Dennis Shipping in Lauderhill. It goes door-to-door to Jamaica, billed by box size, with the customs check at delivery. Ask Dennis for the current transit time. Full Shop & Ship guide.

Is solar cheaper than a gas generator in Jamaica?

To run, yes. An evening outage costs about J$600 in gas for a 3,600 W generator, versus about J$80 to recharge a 2 kWh power station from JPS, or nothing from the sun. A solar generator is also quiet and safe indoors, with no fumes.
